# What a company brain must answer

Eight documents, the same for every business: a crew, a shop, a studio, a practice, a firm. Under each, the questions a new hire would need answered. Every one ends up answered and confirmed by the owner, written down as a gap, or marked as not applying to this business.


## how-we-work

What the business does, where, for whom, how customers reach it, and how a job or sale moves from first contact to paid.

- what the business does and for whom; who the typical customer is
- where the work happens (at the business, at the customer's, online) and how far it serves
- when it works: days, hours, seasons, busy and slow times
- how many people and how the work is split (crews, teams, chairs, departments)
- how customers find the business and reach it: calls, texts, email, walk-ins, website, booking page, referrals
- the business end to end, stage by stage: how a lead arrives, the sale, onboarding, the work, invoicing, and support after; for each stage, who does it and in which tool
- work that repeats on a schedule: weekly, monthly, seasonal, yearly
- how often and when invoices go out, if the business invoices

## tools-and-accounts

Every system the business runs on, who owns each account, where each kind of information lives, and where things break between them.

- each tool: email, phone and texting, calendar or booking, invoicing and payments, bookkeeping, point of sale, files, website, customer list, the software of the trade, AI tools
- for each: what it is used for, who uses it, and whose name the account is in (the business, the owner personally, an outside person)
- where each kind of information lives: customers, the schedule, money, files, notes
- who can log in to what (never the logins themselves)
- what is still on paper, in a spreadsheet, or only in someone's head
- what gets typed twice, and which tools do not talk to each other
- which AI tools are used, by whom, and on which plans

## offer-and-prices

Everything sold, each price with its unit and minimum, what is not offered (and who it is sent to), discounts, deposits, fees, and how quotes work.

- every service, product, package or membership, with its price, unit and minimum
- what the business does not do or sell, and who it sends that work to
- discounts: who gets them and who never does
- deposits, retainers or payment up front
- fees: for cards, travel or distance, estimates, rush work, materials, extra rounds of changes
- how a quote or proposal is made, who signs it, and how long it holds
- the smallest job, order or project the business takes

## policies

Payment terms, late payment, cancellations, returns, refunds and complaints, delays, access and safety, sensitive information, insurance.

- when payment is due and how customers can pay
- what happens when a customer pays late
- cancellations and no-shows: notice needed and any fee
- returns, refunds and complaints: who handles them and who decides
- weather, delays, shortages and rescheduling
- access and safety where the work happens: keys, codes, pets, where such details are kept
- customer information that is sensitive or regulated (health, money, children) and the rules for it
- insurance, licences and certifications
- what is never shared, sent or posted, and who may speak for the business

## people

The owner and what only they decide, each person and their role and days, suppliers, partners and advisors.

- the owner, and what only the owner decides (with any money limit)
- each person: name, role, what they handle, the days they work, who covers for them
- suppliers and partners the business relies on, and how they work with it
- advisors: accountant, lawyer, IT
- who should get which kind of message or question

## customers-and-past-work

Key customers and what to know about each, how many customers, the mix of work, work no longer taken.

- the most important customers or accounts and anything special about each
- how many customers, active projects or regulars there are
- the mix of work or sales (rough shares are fine, marked as estimates)
- kinds of work or customers the business no longer takes, and why
- work the owner is proud of and would point a new customer to

## voice

How the business sounds: tone, greeting and sign-off, words and punctuation used and avoided, promises never made in writing.

- tone and length when writing to customers, by email, text and in replies to reviews
- how messages open and how they are signed
- words, punctuation or styles to avoid
- what is never promised in writing (exact times, dates, outcomes, prices without a quote)
- how the business writes to suppliers, contractors or partners, if different

## decisions

Decisions already made, with when and why: prices changed, services dropped or added, tools changed, lines the business does not cross.

- price changes: what changed, when, from what
- services or products stopped or started: when and why
- tools switched or dropped: when and why
- rules the business set for itself and why (things it will not do)
